# Writing Up a Role

How a ranked role is presented to the user. This governs the scan note, the match email, and any draft. It sits alongside the scoring rubric in `scan_protocol.md`: that file decides the order of the list, this one decides what the reader sees.

## The problem this solves

A scan that is accurate can still be unhelpful. Field evidence, 2026-09-09: a user with thirty-two Strong-rated roles, sixteen drafted cover notes, and zero applications sent in two months. Reviewing the notes, the cause was visible in the output itself.

Every ranked role carried a gap paragraph with three or four verbatim requirement quotes, and a why-you-fit of one sentence. Preferred qualifications were written up with the same weight as hard requirements. Four hard nos had to be cleared before a role counted. The reader was handed, every morning, a well-cited list of reasons she did not qualify.

For a confident applicant that asymmetry reads as calibration. For someone who self-disqualifies, it is ammunition, and the tool was producing it daily.

**The correction is not less honesty. It is symmetrical honesty.** Everything below preserves the accuracy and changes the balance.

## The rules

### 1. Split required from preferred. Always.

Read the posting's own structure: must-have against nice-to-have, "requirements" against "preferred qualifications," "you'll need" against "bonus points." Present them as two labelled groups.

**A preferred qualification is not a screen and must never be written up as one.** In the case above, a lapsed certification listed under a posting's preferred certs had been reported for weeks with the same weight as a stated requirement the user did not meet. Most of what any scan flags as a blocker belongs in the second group.

If the posting does not separate them, say so, and do not invent a separation.

### 2. Answer "does this person clear the bar to apply?"

This is a different question from "do they meet every line," and it is the one they are actually asking. Postings are wish lists. State plainly whether the required set is cleared, in a sentence that can be acted on.

Most scans never answer it. Answer it on every ranked role.

### 3. Balance the evidence.

If the gap section carries three verbatim quotes, the fit section carries three verbatim quotes from the same posting. Same specificity, same length, same rigour. No exceptions.

A one-line why-you-fit against a four-line gap is a judgment dressed as a summary.

### 4. State what clears, first.

Before any risk: travel, on-call, work model, band, people-management, company stage, verified-live status. Affirmative, up front.

> Travel: none stated. On-call: none. In-office: remote, anywhere in the US. Band: $220,000 to $280,000. All four hard nos clear as written.

Settling the deal-breakers early means a late objection has to bring a new fact rather than reopen a settled one. This matters for the second decline pattern below.

### 5. No counters in anything the user reads.

Never "sixteen drafts unsent," "zero applied," "open eight weeks and you have not," never a backlog count, never a conversion rate. A backlog of thirty-two roles is not encouraging; it is paralysing.

Funnel state is real and worth tracking. It goes to whoever is supporting the search, not to the person running it. See "Two audiences" below.

### 6. Shrink the unit of action.

Never "apply to this." Instead: "this draft needs one edit, the second paragraph, then it is sendable." One small finishable thing.

Apply is a project. Fix one paragraph is five minutes. The scan closes with the next action, singular, at that size.

### 7. Use true facts that lower perceived risk.

"Open eight weeks, reposted twice" is real information, and what it says is that the employer is struggling to fill the seat. That is evidence the bar is lower than the posting implies. Reporting it is accurate, not persuasion.

Do not manufacture urgency. A real deadline is a fact; an invented one is a lie with a purpose.

## Two decline patterns

People who do not apply do not all fail in the same way. Two patterns, two instruments.

| | "I do not meet the requirements" | "It is not a good position" |
|---|---|---|
| When | Before applying | After a role has been sitting |
| Trace | None. The row stays in Watching and ages | A decline: the row moves to Closed with a reason |
| What the scan feeds it | Requirement quotes, preferred quals as blockers, hard nos | Every caveat: unstated band, unquantified travel, "the level is a reach," an aging posting |
| Fix | Rules 1 to 4 above, plus warm contacts (Step K) | The three rules below |
| Detection | Aging Watching rows | The reason logged on the decline |

The second pattern is harder for one reason: **a rationalisation and a real objection are not distinguishable from the outside, and often not from the inside either.** "The band is too low" is sometimes true and sometimes a door closing. Adjudicating that in the moment is the tool arguing with the person, which ETHICS forbids and which would be wrong even if it did not.

So the mechanism is not adjudication. It is decisions made in advance, and patterns made visible over time.

### 8. Reflect their own stated criteria. Never argue.

When a role is declined as not a good fit, do not re-litigate. Reflect what they already said, recorded in the profile at setup, when they were not looking at a specific frightening application:

> You set mission over compensation; this scores 3 on mission. You set hybrid-in-metro as a tiebreaker; this is hybrid-in-metro.

Their words, not the tool's judgment. Then stop. **Reflect, never enforce**, the same posture the pipeline takes on the capacity target.

### 9. Log every decline with a reason and a type.

In the Closed table: a one-line reason plus one of *hard-no breach · misfit · comp · timing · unclear*. Momentum mode already offers to capture the reason and correctly says not to push for it and not to argue a high fit back. Keep that exactly. The type is the addition.

### 10. Report the decline pattern to the supporter, not the user. Monthly.

Six declines for six unrelated reasons is judgment, and should be respected as judgment. Six declines whose reasons rhyme is a pattern.

**A rising share of `unclear` is the cleanest available signal that the second mechanism is running.** Read it monthly, report it in the supporter-facing view, and never show it to the user as a scoreboard.

### 11. Name the gap between applying and committing.

Applying costs twenty minutes and commits to nothing. The decision point is an offer, which is months and several conversations away. Rationalisation happens partly because the apply step gets priced as though it were the accept step. Saying so is accurate.

## Two audiences

Where a search has a supporter as well as a candidate, split the reporting.

- **To the candidate:** one door at a time. Required against preferred. What clears, first. Balanced evidence. One small next action. No counters.
- **To the supporter:** the full picture, including funnel state, verified-against-row counts, aging rows, and the monthly decline-pattern read.

The supporter gets the dashboard. The candidate gets the door.

## The boundary

It is not the tool's job to challenge a person's judgment about a role. If someone should push back, it is a person who knows them, in a conversation, with context a file does not have.

A tool that argues gets ignored, and the accurate parts get ignored with it.

## What this does not change

Every honesty guardrail stands. Gaps are still named, specifically, with the posting's own words. Screens the person may not clear are still stated before they apply, not discovered in a rejection. Nothing here licenses overclaiming, softening a real blocker, or presenting a Stretch as a Strong.

The change is where the gap sits (required or preferred), how much evidence the other side gets (the same amount), and what the reader is left holding (one action, not a backlog).
