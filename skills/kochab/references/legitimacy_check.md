# Legitimacy Check

A quick screen on each new posting before it reaches her, so the scan does not send her chasing a ghost job, a scam, or a staffing-firm repost dressed as a direct hire. This runs as part of link verification; it is fast, and most postings pass.

The point is not to be suspicious of everything. It is to catch the few postings that waste an applicant's time or worse, and to flag them honestly rather than rank them as if they were real.

## What to check

- **Is the employer real and currently operating?** A findable company with a real presence, not a shell. For a name you cannot verify at all, say so.
- **Is the req live and recent?** A posting that has been up for months, or that reappears on a cycle, is often a pipeline-filler or a role that is not really open. Note the age if you can find it.
- **Does the comp band make sense?** A band far above or below the market for the title, or an implausibly wide band, is a flag. Not disqualifying, but worth naming.
- **Is this a direct hire or a staffing-firm repost?** Many "postings" are agencies advertising a role they hope to place into. Legitimate, but a different process, and often the same underlying job posted many times. Say which it is.
- **Scam signals.** Asks for payment, personal financial details, or an interview conducted entirely over text/chat app; a domain that mismatches the company; a too-good-to-be-true pitch. These get flagged plainly.

## How to report it

Attach a short line to any posting that is not a clean direct, live, real role. Do not bury a real concern, and do not manufacture one.

- Clean posting: no note needed.
- Aged or recurring: "Posted [date]; has been live a while, may be a pipeline-filler."
- Staffing firm: "This is [agency] advertising the role, not [employer] directly."
- Cannot verify the employer: "Could not confirm this company is currently operating; treat as UNVERIFIED."
- Scam signal: name the specific signal and recommend skipping.

If a posting fails badly enough to be a likely scam, it does not go in the ranked list at all; it goes in a short "screened out as likely not real" note so she can see it was checked.

## Guardrail

A legitimacy flag is a caution, not a verdict, except for clear scams. A staffing-firm repost or an older posting can still be worth a real application. Give her the fact and let her decide.

## Comp sanity check

Public compensation data serves three purposes here: confirming a stated band is plausible, putting a number on an unstated one before a screen, and flagging a band far outside an employer's distribution as a bait or scam marker.

Levels.fyi publishes a machine-readable view at `https://www.levels.fyi/companies/<slug>/salaries.md`, generated live and dated. **Attribution is required**: cite Levels.fyi with a link wherever a figure is used.

State these caveats every time the data is used:

- Self-reported, and **thin or absent for small companies**. No data is not a negative signal.
- Reports **total** compensation, base plus stock plus bonus, while most postings state base only. Never compare the two numbers directly.
- Skews toward engineering titles rather than field, solutions or developer-relations seats.

## Employer reviews as a soft screen

Reviews inform a conversation. They do not decide anything.

Glassdoor is behind bot detection and is not reachable; do not retry it and do not work around it. Three sources cover the same ground:

- **Indeed**, `indeed.com/cmp/<Company>/reviews`. Broadest coverage and the closest shape: an overall rating plus sub-ratings for work-life balance, pay, job security and advancement, management and culture, with dated individual reviews.
- **RepVue**, `repvue.com/companies/<Company>`, case-sensitive slug. Rates sales organisations on culture and leadership, incentive structure, product-market fit, professional development and training, and inbound lead flow, plus verified on-target earnings by role. The most useful of the three for any field or pre-sales seat. **Quota attainment is gated behind contributing a rating: never quote a quota figure unless it is visibly rendered on the page.**
- **Comparably**. Culture score and percentile. Employer-claimed pages skew high; corroboration only, never the primary read.

Rules:

- **A review score never changes a fit score.** The rubric measures fit, not employer quality. Carry it as a separate note on the row.
- **Report sub-scores, not the headline.** One employer's 3.6 overall concealed a 2.8 on job security and advancement, which was the useful number.
- Always state the sample size, and weight recency.
- Never treat an employer-claimed page as independent.
- A first-hand account from someone who worked there outranks any aggregate. Review data generates questions to ask at a screen; it does not issue verdicts.

Where a role is quota-adjacent and the user has asked to de-emphasise sales-oriented work, check RepVue before screening it out on that basis alone. A sales organisation in a high percentile for professional development and training is evidence of the carve-out such a preference usually allows.
